Find what's coming

Cover something before the wave reaches YouTube.

7→ b → signals/reddit_signals.md

Terms climbing fast on Reddit but thin on YouTube. Treat these as leads to check, not forecasts — the idea that social chatter reliably precedes YouTube is widely repeated and, as far as I know, never actually demonstrated.

Free · no YouTube quota

See what actually moved

Is this niche going anywhere since you last looked?

9compare → trends/since_last_build.md

The only measured demand figure the engine produces. Everywhere else `views_per_hour` is a lifetime average, so a video that did 400k views in its first week and nothing since reports a healthy number forever. Two snapshots give actual views gained — which separates still being watched from finished months ago. Needs two builds of the same niche, days apart.

Free · reads the history/ folder

See which themes actually last

Is this format durable, or was it a moment?

3discover → breakouts/by_era.md

Breakouts split into 0-90d / 90-180d / 180-365d, each ranked against its own contemporaries. A theme appearing in all three eras is a format; one appearing only in the newest is a news cycle. Fixes an age bias where recent videos took 43 of the top 50 and the single biggest video in the dataset was invisible.

Free · slices the build you already have

Mine the Shorts you filtered out

A third of every build is Shorts. What were they about?

3discover → shorts/shorts.md

Subjects busy in short form but thin in long-form — attention exists, nobody made the full version. Leads, not findings: a Short's views come from the feed, not from search, so short-form attention doesn't prove anyone would search for a ten-minute version. Written automatically; costs nothing extra.

Free · data was already collected

Find a repeatable format

A series, not a one-off. At a small channel size this matters more than any single video decision here.

3discover → breakouts/breakout_clusters.csv → sort by unique_channels

A cluster where seven or more separate channels succeeded is a format, not a fluke. That's the difference between one good video and a content engine — your second video benefits from the first in a way one-off topics never allow.

Free after the build

What it can't do

Worth knowing before you go looking for these.

Compare two niches head to head
Option 5 grades a niche against its own baseline, which forces demand to about 64 and supply to exactly 50. That's arithmetic, not information. Cross-niche grading exists in the code but isn't wired into the menu — for now, run option 3 on each and compare cluster strength by hand.
Tell you whether your video will work
It never sees your channel. Every number describes the market, not your odds in it.
Judge whether a video is on-topic
There's no topical relevance filter, deliberately. Occasional off-topic leaks are the price of not silently dropping things that only look irrelevant.
Measure current demand from one build
A single snapshot cannot separate a video still climbing from one that stopped a year ago — views_per_hour is a lifetime average. Option 9 fixes this, but only once you have two builds of the same niche to compare.
Measure search demand in the scoring
Scores are built from how existing videos performed. A topic nobody has covered looks identical to a topic nobody wants. Option 7 gets close from the outside, but it doesn't feed the scoring yet.
See why a video worked
Click-through rate, average view duration, retention and traffic sources are private analytics. No public API exposes them at any price, for any channel. Everything here is inference from outside.
